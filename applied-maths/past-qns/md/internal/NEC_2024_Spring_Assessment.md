#### 1.
a) Show that $u = e^x (x \cos y - y \sin y)$ is a harmonic function. Find the analytic function for which $u$ is the imaginary part. **[8]**

b) State Cauchy's Integral Formula. Evaluate:

$$
\oint_C \frac{z+1}{z^3 - 2z^2} \, dz
$$

where $C$ is the circle $|z| = 1$, counter-clockwise. **[7]**

---

#### 2.
a) Find the bilinear transformation which maps the points $z = 0, -1, i$ onto $w = i, 0, \infty$. Also find the image of the unit circle $|z| = 1$. **[8]**

**OR**

Find the Laurent series for $f(z) = \frac{7z-2}{(z+1)(z-3)}$ in the regions:
1. $0 < |z+1| < 1$
2. $1 < |z+1| < 3$  
**[8]**

b) State Cauchy's Residue Theorem. By applying it, evaluate:

$$
\oint_C \frac{z-3}{z^2(z-2)} \, dz
$$

where $C$ is the circle $|z| = 5$. **[7]**

---

#### 3.
a) State and prove the first shifting theorem of Z-transform. Obtain the inverse Z-transform:

$$
Z^{-1}\left[ \frac{3z^2 - 18z + 26}{(z-2)(z-3)^2} \right]
$$

**[8]**

b) Using Z-transform and inverse Z-transform, solve the difference equation:

$$
y_{n+2} - 2y_{n+1} + y_n = 2^n
$$

with $y_0 = 2$, $y_1 = 1$. **[7]**

---

#### 4.
a) Show that:

$$
\int_0^\infty \frac{\sin w \cos xw}{w} \, dw = \begin{cases} 
\frac{\pi}{2}, & 0 \le x < 1 \\ 
\frac{\pi}{4}, & x = 1 \\ 
0, & x > 1 
\end{cases}
$$

**[8]**

b) Find the Fourier transform of $e^{-|x|}$, and hence evaluate the integral:

$$
\int_0^\infty \frac{x \sin mx}{1 + x^2} \, dx
$$

**[7]**

---

#### 5.
a) Derive the one-dimensional heat equation with necessary assumptions. **[8]**

**OR**

Using the method of separation of variables, solve:

$$
\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} = \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2} + u
$$

where $u(x, 0) = 6e^{-3x}$. **[8]**

b) A tightly stretched string with fixed end points $x = 0$ and $x = l$ is initially at rest in its equilibrium position. If it is set vibrating by giving to each of its points a velocity $v(x) = kx(l - x)$, find the displacement of the string at any distance $x$ from one end at any time $t$. **[7]**

#### 6.
a) Solve the Laplace equation:

$$
\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial y^2} = 0
$$

which satisfies the boundary conditions $u(0, y) = u(l, y) = u(x, 0) = 0$ and $u(x, a) = \sin\left(\frac{\pi x}{l}\right)$. **[8]**

b) Solve the Laplace equation in polar coordinates:

$$
\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial r^2} + \frac{1}{r} \frac{\partial u}{\partial r} + \frac{1}{r^2} \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial \theta^2} = 0
$$

**[7]**
