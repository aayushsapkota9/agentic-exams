# Applied Mathematics Syllabus

**Course Description:**
This course is designed for developing competency of the students in the applications of various mathematical concepts they learned in courses in previous semesters. It is equipped with complex analysis, Z-transform, Partial differential Equations and Fourier Transform. The prerequisite for this course is Calculus I, II and Algebra and Geometry. The course will be delivered through lecture method, assignment on practically base engineering problems and class tests.

**General Objectives:**
* To acquaint the students with applications of mathematical tools in engineering.

**Methods of Instruction:**
Lecture, tutorials, discussions and assignments

---

## Contents in Detail

### Unit I: Complex Analysis (17 Hours)
**Specific Objective:** Understand and apply function of complex variables, Calculus of functions of complex variables and their applications in Engineering problems.
* **1.1 Complex numbers and functions (5 hrs.)**
  * 1.1.1 Review on Complex number, their geometric representation, Polar form, power and roots.
  * 1.1.2 Sets and functions in complex plane, Limits Continuity and derivatives of function of complex variables (Definition and concepts only).
  * 1.1.3 Analytic functions, Cauchy-Riemann(C-R) equations as necessary conditions for functions to be analytic, C-R equations as sufficient condition for analyticity (without proof), Polar form of C-R equations (No derivation).
  * 1.1.4 Laplace equation, harmonic functions and harmonic conjugate.
  * 1.1.5 Related problems.
* **1.2 Integrals in complex plane (4 hrs.)**
  * 1.2.1 Line integrals in the complex plane, Evaluation of basic line integrals in complex plane.
  * 1.2.2 Cauchy's Integral theorem, Cauchy's integral formula and Cauchy integral formula of higher order (for analytic functions) without proof.
  * 1.2.3 Related problems.
* **1.3 Taylor and Laurent series for functions of complex variables. (6 hrs.)**
  * 1.3.1 Taylor series and Laurent series (Without Proof) and Related Problems.
  * 1.3.2 Singularities and zeros, Residues and integration, Cauchy Residue theorem (Without proof) and related Problems.
* **1.4 Conformal mapping (2 hrs.)**
  * 1.4.1 Special Linear fractional transformation (Bilinear fractional transformation) only.

### Unit II: Z-Transform and its Applications (10 Hours)
**Specific Objective:** Understand and apply discrete transforms and solve difference equations.
* **2.1 Z-transform introduction (4 hrs.)**
  * 2.1.1 Z-transform, Z-transform of elementary functions, Properties of Z-transforms, Shifting theorems, initial value theorem, final value theorem.
* **2.2 Inverse Z-transform (3 hrs.)**
  * 2.2.1 Inverse z-transforms using division method, expansion method, Partial fraction method and residue method.
* **2.3 Difference Equations (3 hrs.)**
  * 2.3.1 Application: Difference equations and solution by using Z-transform.

### Unit III: Partial Differential Equations (12 Hours)
**Specific Objective:** Understand and apply higher dimensional systems and describe them by partial differential equations with solution techniques and interpretation of solutions.
* **3.1 Introduction (3 hrs.)**
  * 3.1.1 Partial differential equations and solutions by variable separation method.
* **3.2 One dimensional wave equation (3 hrs.)**
  * 3.2.1 One dimensional wave equation and its solutions and related problems.
* **3.3 One dimensional heat equation (3 hrs.)**
  * 3.3.1 One dimensional heat equation and its solutions and related problems.
* **3.4 Two dimensional equations (3 hrs.)**
  * 3.4.1 Two dimensional heat equation, Laplace equation (steady state heat equation) and its solution for rectangular boundaries. Laplace equation in polar form and its solution for circular boundaries, related problems.

### Unit IV: Fourier integral and Transform (6 Hours)
**Specific Objective:** Evaluate Fourier integrals and Transforms.
* **4.1 Fourier integrals (3 hrs.)**
  * 4.1.1 Fourier integral, Fourier sine and cosine integrals and related problems.
* **4.2 Fourier transforms (3 hrs.)**
  * 4.2.1 Fourier integral in complex form and Fourier transform and inverse transform, Fourier sine and cosine transforms and their inverse transforms, Convolution theorem, Parseval’s identity and related problems.

*Note: The figures in the parentheses indicate the approximate periods for the respective units.*
