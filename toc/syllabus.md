**Program:** BE (Computer)  
**Lecture:** 3 hrs | **Tutorial:** 1 hr | **Practical:** 0 hr

---

## Course Description
This course is designed to provide basic knowledge of the theory of automata, formal languages and computational complexity.

## General Objectives
* To acquaint the students with the basic knowledge of automata and formal languages.
* To develop the skills in students to design various types of automata and analyze them.
* To acquaint the students with the concepts of computability, computational bounds and computational complexity.

## Methods of Instruction
Lecture, Discussion, Readings, Tutorials.

---

## Contents in Detail

### Unit 1: Introduction (4 Hours)
**Specific Objective:** Understand the concept of alphabet and language.
* **1.1 Review of set, relation and function**
* **1.2 Proof techniques** – proof by contradiction, pigeon hole principle, induction and diagonalization.
* **1.3 Alphabets and language**
* **1.4 Chomsky’s hierarchy**

### Unit 2: Finite Automata and Regular Language (10 Hours)
**Specific Objectives:** 
- Design and implement deterministic and non-deterministic finite automata.
- Develop the equivalence of regular languages and finite automata.
* **2.1 Deterministic Finite Automata, Non-Deterministic Finite Automata**
* **2.2 Regular expressions and regular language, equivalence of regular language and finite automata**
* **2.3 Properties of regular language**
* **2.4 Pumping lemma for regular sets**
* **2.5 Closure properties of regular sets**
* **2.6 Decision algorithms for regular sets**

### Unit 3: Context-Free Language and Pushdown Automata (13 Hours)
**Specific Objectives:** 
- Explain the theory and design of context-free grammar and pushdown automata and their equivalence.
- Explore derivation trees, simplification and formal forms of context-free grammar.
* **3.1 Context-free grammar**
* **3.2 Derivation trees and simplification of context-free grammar**
* **3.3 Normal forms (CNF, GNF)**
* **3.4 Pushdown automata** (formal description and final state PDA design)
* **3.5 Equivalence of pushdown automata and context-free grammar**
* **3.6 Properties of context-free languages (CFL)**
* **3.7 Pumping lemma for CFL’s**
* **3.8 Closure properties of CFL’s**
* **3.9 Decision algorithms for CFL’s**

### Unit 4: Turing Machines (10 Hours)
**Specific Objectives:** 
- Explain the theory and significance of Turing machines.
- Explain computing mechanism and extensions of Turing machines.
- Understand computable languages, functions and unrestricted grammar.
* **4.1 Introduction to Turing machine**
* **4.2 Computing with Turing machine**
* **4.3 Extensions of Turing machine**
* **4.4 Unrestricted grammar**
* **4.5 Recursively enumerable languages**

### Unit 5: Undecidability (4 Hours)
**Specific Objectives:** 
- Use the idea of undecidability introducing Church-Turing thesis and the halting problem.
- Understand universal Turing machines and undecidable problems of Turing machines.
* **5.1 The Church-Turing thesis**
* **5.2 Halting Problem**
* **5.3 Universal Turing machines**
* **5.4 Undecidable problems about Turing machines**
* **5.5 Properties of Recursive and Recursively enumerable languages**

### Unit 6: Computational Complexity (4 Hours)
**Specific Objective:** Understand the concept of computational complexity and different classes of problems.
* **6.1 Introduction to Complexity theory, tractable and intractable problems**
* **6.2 Class P and Class NP problems**
* **6.3 NP-complete problems**

## List of Tutorials (15 Hours)

The following tutorial activities of 15 hours per group of maximum 24 students should be conducted to cover the content of this course:

| Category | Topics / Activities | Tutorial Hours |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **A. Discussion-based Tutorials** | 1. Review discussion on set theory concepts like functions, relations, etc.<br>2. Discussion on the theoretical meaning and significance of computation and computers.<br>3. Study and presentation on the history of computation and Alan Turing as the father of computation (Oral Presentation). | **2 hrs** |
| **B. Problem solving-based Tutorials** | 1. Solve problems to design deterministic and non-deterministic finite automata (DFA and NDFA) to recognize/generate given regular languages.<br>2. Develop regular expressions to recognize/generate given regular languages.<br>3. Convert a given NDFA to DFA.<br>4. Design Context-Free Grammar (CFG) to recognize/generate given Context-Free Language (CFL).<br>5. Simplify a given CFG and convert it into CNF and GNF.<br>6. Design Pushdown Automata (PDA) to recognize/generate given Context-Free Language (CFL).<br>7. Design Turing machines to recognize/generate given languages. | **8 hrs** |
| **C. Review & Q/A-based Tutorials** | 1. Case study of examples of DFA, PDA and Turing Machines for some practical tasks followed by Oral Presentation/demonstration in class.<br>2. Case study of Unsolvable problems such as the Tiling problem followed by Oral Presentation in class.<br>3. Case study of Class NP and NP-complete problems (e.g. Travelling Salesman Problem) followed by Oral Presentation in class. | **5 hrs** |
